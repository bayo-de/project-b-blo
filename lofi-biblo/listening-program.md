# The Listening Program

How Lo Fi Biblo went to school on a reference stream, October 2026.

When V6 became the blueprint, we knew its sound from the inside. What we
did not know was how our instincts compared to the music people actually
live inside for hours. So for the whole of October 2026, several times a
day, Biblo sat with one beloved lofi reference stream and listened on
purpose: ten minutes at a time, measured, written down, and argued with.

73 full listening sessions by the end. This page is the method and what
it taught us.

## The rule

Learn what makes it great. Never copy it.

We measured technicalities only: tempo, key, how chords move, how the
frequency balance sits, how the dynamics breathe, how long sections
last. No melodies were transcribed. Nothing here is the reference
stream's music. It is a description of its habits, the way a student
describes a master's posture without tracing his hand.

## The method

Each session followed the same cycle.

1. **Capture.** Ten or more minutes of the stream, taken as found.
2. **Analyze.** Tempo, estimated key, chord transitions, frequency
   balance in bands, dynamics, and section lengths, computed from the
   audio itself.
3. **Journal.** Every session written up the same day, and every claim
   in the journal checked against that session's own analysis file.
   When a report and the file disagreed, the file won.
4. **Aggregate.** Findings only counted when they repeated. A pattern
   seen once was labeled tentative and carried no weight. A pattern seen
   three times was promoted to validated and could change the stream
   plan. Short captures under five minutes were excluded by a fixed
   rule, set before we saw their contents.

That discipline is the real methodology. Anyone can listen. The work is
refusing to believe a thing until it has happened three times, and
keeping the receipts when it has.

## What 73 sessions taught us

**Tempo.** The reference lives slower than we guessed and steadier than
we feared. Felt tempo ranged from 52 to 99, with the great bulk between
62 and 83, and both the median and the most common value at 72. Our own
baseline of 76 BPM sits inside that pocket, a touch above center. We
kept it, and we stopped worrying about it.

**Harmony.** Major keys anchor first on the move between the tonic and
the fourth chord, and second on tonic to fifth. Mediant color shows up
in about half of the major sessions, and borrowed, chromatic color in
most of them: the plain progression is the exception, not the rule.
Minor keys rotate through four anchor pulls, tonic to fourth, tonic to
fifth, tonic to the flat third, and tonic to the flat sixth, each one
validated across the month. The lesson for our writing: one strong
anchor, colored freely.

**Mix.** Dark and warm is not a taste. It is the measurable norm. Session
after session, the energy of the reference sits low, with the great
majority of its weight below 800 Hz and only two genuinely bright
readings in the entire month. Our dark, warm mix direction, which began
as Bayo's instinct, ended the month as a measured fact about the music
people stay with.

**Form.** Sections run about thirty seconds. Nothing hurries. The
stream trusts a small room of ideas and lets people live in it.

## What changed, and what did not

The program validated more than it overturned. The tempo stayed. The
mix stayed. The harmonic anchors the engine already wrote with turned
out to be the anchors the reference lives on. What changed is the
standing of those choices: they are no longer preferences we hope are
right. They are preferences the evidence keeps.

Two things remain open, honestly. We have not yet run the same
measurement battery over our own V6 render to compare it band for band
against these norms, and the dynamics comparison is still to come. When
those are done, they will be written here the same way: measured first,
believed second.
