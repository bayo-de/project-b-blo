# Lo Fi Biblo

The main line of Project B-Blo. Warm, dreamy lofi hip hop: Rhodes-style
keys, stepping pads, sparse motifs, straight boom-bap drums. Smooth and
enveloping, made for long listening. Every track is judged against the
V6 mix.

## Tracks

**V6** (0:25, seamless loop) — `tracks/v6.wav`

The blueprint. 76 BPM in C major: Dm9, G13, Cmaj9, Am9. Warm Rhodes with
long sustains, stepping pads and the embrace layer, sub you feel more
than hear, two shimmer pings per cycle, a two-note motif that asks and
answers. Drums never swing the snare. This is the mix every B-Blo track
is judged against.

## V7: what we learned between V6 and V7

V6 was the blueprint. Warm Rhodes, 76 BPM, C major, smooth and
enveloping. It became the mix every B-Blo track is judged against.

Then, at 3 AM on October 2, 2026, Bayo gave the note that changed
everything: the music "sounds like a computer made it." He was right.
So we went to school.

**Step one: name the tells.** We listed exactly what made the engine
sound mechanical. Random timing jitter on every hit. Velocity rolled
like dice instead of shaped like a phrase. Swing as one global knob.
Kick drum locked to the grid. Bass playing roots on the one. A new
motif generated for every section instead of one idea developed.
Chords jumping in blocks with no voice leading. Perfect polish, no
mistakes. Flat arrangements. Static dynamics. Ten tells, written down
plain.

**Step two: study the masters.** Dilla, the godfather of lofi.
Nujabes, Tomppabeats, Madlib, Knxwledge. Not to copy them. Bayo's rule:
"Let them inspire you. Don't copy. Learn what makes them great, then
be great. Be original. Be excellent." We studied what they actually do.
Dilla's feel is one committed micro-timing map, repeated identically,
never re-rolled. Madlib leaves a deliberate mistake in and moves on.
Nujabes states an idea naked, then builds, strips, and returns.

**Step three: the feel study.** We rebuilt the groove from those
principles. One fixed feel template per track, committed for the whole
song. Velocity that follows the phrase: starts lift, pickups rise,
endings decay. Straight hats leaning against an early snare, the
tension living between the voices. A quiet stutter kick pushing into
every backbeat. Bass that answers the kick, lazy behind the beat,
instead of droning roots. One seed motif, exposed plain, then flipped,
sequenced, inverted, and brought home. Chords chosen by voice leading,
each voicing a small step from the last. One deliberate mistake per
section, placed and left alone. Arrangements that expose then flip.
And sidechain breathe: the pads duck gently under every kick so the
whole track breathes with the drums.

**Step four: find the voice.** The Rhodes melody had gone whiny and
played out, too high and too familiar. So we auditioned four new
melody voices over the same feel study: piano, harp, plucked strings,
handpan. Then brightened all four, because the first pass sat too far
back in the mix.

**Step five: Bayo's ears.** He approved V7 as a solid elevation of
quality. It is now the quality bar. Next: he picks the winning melody
voice, and the feel system ports into the main engine.

That is the whole journey. A blueprint, an honest critique, a list of
tells, the masters, a study, four voices, and one pair of ears.
