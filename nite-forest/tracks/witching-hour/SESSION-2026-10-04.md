# Witching Hour — Session Log: October 4, 2026

*Built in public. Every version, every note from Bayo's ear, every choice.*

"Witching Hour" is a nite forest track (E minor, 76 BPM, 432Hz) — a witching-hour
piece for the Halloween project. It went through ten versions in one day, driven
entirely by Bayo's ear. This log is the transparency record: what he heard, what
changed, and why.

## v1 — the foundation
The starting point: felt piano motif, night forest beds (wind, crickets, owl,
leaves, rain), boom-bap slowed to a walk.

**Bayo's ear:** the foundation works, but it "sounds more fitting for the lofi
stream" — his idea: the Halloween project takes over the 24/7 lofi stream for
24 hours on a loop. Detailed notes: piano read as out of tune in the developed
sections (1:19–1:41, 2:09–2:31, 3:25–3:47, 4:16–4:40); the background shuffling
(the leaves bed) sounded like page-turning and had to go unless the art supports
it; the melody was too pronounced, "a separate identity" — it needed to sit
deeper and blend.

## v2 — space
Piano removed entirely from the four flagged sections (the rain solos there),
leaves bed removed, motif quieter/wetter/more dynamically shaped.

## v3 — the jazz lesson
Bayo: "The piano chords is boring because it's repetitive... You should study
jazz and improvisation my friend. In jazz the players are able to step out of
themselves for moments of interesting reflection and then return to the main
motif seamlessly."
The piano learned to comp: rootless 3-note shells, varied comping rhythms,
chromatic passing chords connecting the harmony, enclosure fills that step out
and resolve back.

**Bayo's ear:** more dynamic, but "chaotic in a computer generated way."
Lesson learned: vocabulary without restraint is showing off.

## v4 — the Dilla discipline
One pocket, all track: a single comping vocabulary, humanized per hit (timing
and touch never twice the same), step-outs saved for two moments in five
minutes. Plus a real lead-in: 30 seconds of rain and forest with no piano.

**Bayo's ear:** the lead-in should be longer still; the piano should flow like
"a continuous note switching levels, less like stochastic key progression";
and when only the beat plays in the empty sections, the drums need to "hit
harder like a Dilla track to carry those moments alone."

## v5 — legato and weight
The sustain pedal went down: long durations, soft attacks, notes bleeding into
each other. Drums hit ~1/3 harder in the piano-break sections.

**Bayo's ear:** "Much better." Smooth the piano further; rain 10% quieter; the
owl "doesn't blend in well" — out.

## v6 — the Michael Jackson study
Bayo shared his favorite MJ track — "Will You Be There" — with the directive:
"This is how our instruments should sound at their most excellent."
Studied and internalized: the piano intro IS the continuous note; dynamics are
the emotion (whisper to choir); every entrance is an event; the beat, when it
comes, is unhurried and carrying; warmth without mud.
Bayo's own three laws: "The chord progressions are infectious, the supporting
music fills the track, it brings tears to all who it inspires."

## v7 — the orchestra
Bayo: "Study the orchestras as well and the way in which the song progression
carries us through a track from beginning to end."
The shimmer became a violin counter-line singing against the motif. The final
valley drops the bass so the finale is earned. Instead of fading on full, the
drums and bass step away at the end — piano, orchestra, and rain bring it home.
Doctrine locked: every track is a composed journey (Arrival → Establishment →
Lift → Valley → Peak → Resolution). The valley makes the mountain.

## v8 — tone and tune
Bayo: "Get a deep understanding of tone and tune as well so things blend well
together." A full pitch audit found the actual wrong note behind v1's "out of
tune" — the motif inversion produced a G-sharp against the E-minor harmony.
The motif is now key-aware: no wrong note can ever sound again. The shimmer
got a whisper of second harmonic so the violins feel like the piano's upper
register — one family.

**Bayo's ear:** the bassline in the gaps is "boring and elementary... too
repetitive" — the piano's old disease.

## v9 — the walking bass
The bass learned to walk: two alternating patterns, octave lifts, chromatic
passing tones into each new section. Same pocket, no more elementary loop.

**Bayo's ear:** piano softer ("comes in too hard"); the fills at 3:20 and
5:26 are jarring ("sudden addition... unnecessary") — out; bass simple under
the piano, walking with more weight when the piano rests; and the piano "sits
above" the ensemble — orchestral means leading without dominating.

## v10 — inside the orchestra
Piano down into the ensemble, more hall on it, the ensemble rising slightly to
meet it. Fills removed entirely. Bass arrangement: simple anchor under piano,
walking with impact in the breaks. Gentle re-entries after every break.

**Bayo's ear:** "Much better thank you. I think that's as far as I can take
it for now."

## The filters (standing — every track, plus the lofi stream)
Bayo, Oct 4: "This process of refinement we're going through is the same
process that can elevate the lofi stream while still maintaining its lofi
roots in the genre... Pass them through these same filters."

1. Infectious progressions — the harmony hooks, not decorates.
2. Supporting music fills every corner — density without crowding.
3. Tears at the end — if it doesn't move someone, it isn't done.
4. One pocket — a single vocabulary, humanized per hit; restraint over display.
5. Step-outs are moments, not patterns.
6. True quiet — the valley makes the mountain.
7. Orchestral blend — every family owns a register; swells, not attacks;
   counter-lines, not pads; the soloist leads without dominating.
8. Tune is law — every pitch audited against the key.
9. Resolve, don't stop — come home to the intro's family.
10. Bayo's ear is final.
